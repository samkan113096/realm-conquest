Chronicle I

four independent game worlds

When the last emperor invoked the Seal of Unity, the empire did not die — it split. Continents drifted into pocket dimensions. Stars peeled into debris lanes. Catacombs sank beneath borders that no longer met. What remained was not peace, but a thousand wars for the shape of tomorrow.

The Sundering

For ten thousand years, the Eternal Empire ruled a single contiguous realm — every road connected, every oath sworn to the Obsidian Throne. Archmages called it perfection. Historians call it hubris. When Emperor Valdris III faced rebellion on every border, he ordered the Seal of Unity: magic that would fuse all dissent into loyalty.

Instead, reality buckled. Mountains folded into hexagonal shards. Fleets were flung into neon-lit voids between fragments. The undercities tore loose and fell into endless dungeon strata. The empire became four independent game worlds — an ancient civilization split into warring fragments, each convinced it alone held the true succession.

Fragment I — The Hex Marches (Realm Conquest)

On the surface shards, land reorganized into perfect hex territories — as if some cosmic hand wanted war to be fair and therefore endless. Noble houses woke to find their castles centered on single tiles, their armies scattered across neutral ground.

You are one of these lords: a minor house with a claim no larger than a village, and ambition larger than the old empire. In Realm Conquest, you reclaim hex territories from rivals and neutrals alike. Farms feed your people; barracks forge your spears; markets fund your dreams. Every border you paint is a vote for which fragment might one day host the reunited throne.

The other lords do not sleep. AI-driven houses expand, starve, and betray with the same hunger you feel. The Marches teach a brutal lesson: the empire was not shattered by malice alone, but by the certainty that your piece of the map is the whole world.

Fragment II — The Debris Lanes (Neon Drift)

Above the shards, the sky broke into rivers of wreckage — palace domes, war frigates, cathedral spires, all spinning in cobalt and magenta radiation. The void between fragments is not empty; it is a graveyard with velocity.

Neon Drift follows the void pilots who refused to touch land again. They race through the standalone game's cosmic debris in patched corvettes, weaving asteroid fields for sport and survival. To them, the Sundering was liberation: no emperors, only momentum. Combos chain across hull scrapes; power-ups bloom in wreckage like coral. Permadeath is the price of freedom — and the reason every run must be faster than the last.

Rumors persist of a intact vault-ship at the lane's center holding the emperor's astrolabe. No pilot has returned from that bearing. The ones who come closest speak of a hum that sounds like the Seal, still trying to unify what cannot be glued.

Fragment III — The Underrealm (Shadow Strike)

When continents split, what lived beneath did not die — it deepened. Catacombs, prisons, and throne-rooted dungeons collapsed into a vertical empire beneath the Marches: the Underrealm. Light is a rumor here. Oaths are written in blood on wet stone.

The assassin clans of Shadow Strike were once the emperor's quiet hands. Now they answer no crown. They descend floor by floor, fighting corrupted wardens, void-touched beasts, and the echoes of kings buried alive during the Sundering. Touch controls and combo chains are not mere mechanics; they are the grammar of survival when every hallway might be your tomb.

Bosses every five floors are not monsters — they are fragment-anchors, magical keystones holding shards apart. Destroy one, and seismologists on the surface report a border shifting. The assassins know: eventually someone will strike the final anchor, and either the realm reunites or everything falls into the dark forever.

Fragment IV — The Chain Bridges (Realm GameFi)

Not all fractures were physical. When the Seal shattered, it sprayed REALM — crystallized intent — across a new plane: the Chain Bridges on Optimism. Here, value flows like blood between fragments. Play conquest, drift, or shadow and your deeds mint proof. Proof becomes token. Token becomes stake, trade, and hero NFTs called RHERO.

Realm GameFi is the lifeblood connecting all fragments. Lords, pilots, and assassins rarely meet in flesh, but their wallets speak a common tongue. The REALM token is not greed — it is the empire's nervous system trying to knit itself back together through play to earn. Web3 gaming on L2 is fast enough that rewards feel like loot, not paperwork.

Archivists debate: is GameFi the healing, or a fifth fragment that will swallow the other four? The smart contracts do not argue. They only settle.

Convergence — One throne, four paths

Prophecy — or propaganda — claims that when a lord holds every hex, a pilot charts the vault-ship, an assassin breaks the final anchor, and a staker holds one million REALM, the Obsidian Throne will reappear in the void between. None have succeeded. Many have died trying.

That is why Realm Games offers four free paths into the same war: strategy on land, speed in the sky, violence below, and economy on chain. four independent game worlds does not ask which fragment you love. It asks whether you will fight to make it whole.
